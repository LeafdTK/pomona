Pomona

One page every morning, written from what you're actually in the middle of.

Your server

Looking for your Pomona server…

Choose a passphrase. Everything the server stores is encrypted with it: your briefs, your source tokens, all of it. There is no way to recover it, so keep it somewhere.

Opens the server's sign-in in a small window: Slack, or a code by email. Nothing to copy.

Signed in on another browser? Its settings page can show a six digit code under "Pair another browser". Type it here once and this browser is paired for good.

Every paired browser holds a key to this account. Signing the others out ends them all and leaves this one working. Deleting your briefs removes every stored page, note and excerpt for this account and keeps your connections. Deleting the account removes those too, and every browser's key, at once. Pomona keeps clipped excerpts of what it read for three days and the pages it wrote for as long as you choose below; here is the whole of it.

What Pomona remembers

Notes it kept from past mornings, stored encrypted on your server and nowhere else. They go into each new brief so it can tell what you cleared from what you've been carrying all week. Delete anything you'd rather it forgot.

    What you own

    Worked out from where you post, what you merge, and what you made, and recomputed every morning. A question in one of these with no answer is yours to answer. Nothing here is asked for; anything wrong is one click to take back.

      Places you've silenced

      Channels and repositories Pomona stops reading altogether. Silencing happens before anything is gathered, so a muted place costs nothing and can never argue its way back into a brief. Direct messages can't be silenced.

        These faded on their own. Pomona showed them to you every morning for weeks and you never once opened, ticked or acted on one, so it stopped gathering them. Bring any of them back and it starts over with a clean slate.

          Connecting apps

          Do this once and everyone on this server connects Slack with a click, with no tokens to copy. Open your Slack app's Basic Information page, add the redirect URL below under OAuth & Permissions, then paste the two values from App Credentials. Slack has no API for these, which is why it's the one thing you have to copy by hand.

          Sources

          Everything is read-only, stored encrypted on your server, and sent only to Claude when the brief is written.

          You

          Claude writes to a person, not an inbox. Most of this is read from what you connected, so it should already be right. Fix anything that isn't.

          Anything else

          Any URL that returns JSON, RSS, or text: a status page, a changelog, a Notion query, your own API. Give it a name and Claude gets it as one more labelled source.

          Claude

          The server runs Claude for you. On a subscription it drives Claude Code, so every model is available and your plan covers it; on a server that isn't your machine that takes a token from `claude setup-token`. With an API key it calls the Messages API instead.

          Every morning

          It starts reading twenty minutes before the hour, so the page is finished when you open the browser.
